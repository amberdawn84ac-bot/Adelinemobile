# Dear Adeline game art direction

## North star

A vintage field-journal world with a little magic in the seams. Warm, tactile, hand-built, slightly imperfect, and grounded enough for older kids. Jewel tones appear as punctuation, not wallpaper.

## Visual language

- parchment, graphite, ink, paper grain, weathered wood, denim, brass, moss, clay
- fine doodle-like marks and imperfect geometry
- realistic town places rather than subject-themed fantasy buildings
- selective saturated accents: garnet, sapphire, emerald, amethyst, amber, peacock
- botanical and natural-history illustration influence
- ordinary objects can hide strange or impossible worlds
- no glossy rainbow education-app aesthetic
- no neon gamer chrome unless a specific story beat calls for it

## Product rule

If removing XP, coins, labels, and academic language makes the experience boring, it is not yet a game.

The player should experience a place, a problem, a clue, a person, an object, weather, a decision, or something worth building. Academic tracking stays underneath the world.

## UI rule

Use the journal metaphor for records, progress, portfolio, and long-term tracking. Avoid exposing school-system language during normal play.

## Tone

Curious, grounded, intelligent, lightly strange. Never babyish, breathlessly motivational, or fake-cool.
