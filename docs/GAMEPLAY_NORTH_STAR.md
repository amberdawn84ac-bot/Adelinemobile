# Gameplay north star

## Core loop

Explore → notice → investigate → use a skill → make/test/decide → world reacts → keep the evidence.

## The town is not a subject menu

Buildings are believable places. The ten Dear Adeline tracks run invisibly underneath experiences across the town.

A creek problem can involve chemistry, math, writing, economics, government, history, and stewardship without ever displaying those subjects to the player.

## First-play test

A child should be able to play for twenty minutes without anyone explaining the educational purpose and still want to continue.

## Avoid

- “How do you want to learn?”
- quiz/story/build mode language as the primary experience
- emoji buildings
- constant XP, coin, transcript, mastery, or graduation messaging
- motivational filler
- canned praise
- school dashboards inside normal play
- every interaction becoming a text box

## Build toward

- environmental clues
- weather and world events
- believable NPC motives
- tools and physical interactions
- repair/build systems
- collection and discovery
- cooperative play
- player-owned spaces
- consequences that persist
- hidden academic evidence capture

## Adeline

Adeline is a person in the world, not a mascot and not a floating tutor. She can know the learner through the Brain, but her presence should feel like a trusted older person at the kitchen table, workshop, garden, or town rather than an AI assistant panel.
